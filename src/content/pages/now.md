# Now

_Last updated: August 13, 2026_

I’m currently focused on the overlap between manufacturing, software, international business, and practical infrastructure.

A lot of my work sits in the middle layer: making technical capability easier to understand, turning messy business processes into clearer systems, and using software to make real-world operations less fragile.

## What I’m working on

### Precision fluid control for extreme conditions

I’m helping a manufacturing company reposition itself around precision fluid control platforms for extreme conditions.

The product lines that anchor this are more concrete than they sound: solenoid valves for dispensers, hydronic modules for wall-hung boilers, city gas regulators, camshafts, sheathed thermocouples, and electric heaters. The expansion list adds pressure and flow sensors.

The engineering story behind these components — reliability under temperature, pressure, and continuous duty — is where the real value sits. The job is to make that story legible to customers, to the market, and to the company itself.

### Industrial companies going global

I’m working on how Chinese manufacturing companies can present themselves more clearly to international customers.

Recently this has meant defining a multi-category overseas positioning — valves, mechanical parts, and instruments all need to be understandable under one coherent brand — plus the hiring profile for an overseas market manager who can own that story end to end.

There’s a competitive angle too: global giants in fluid control and valves often under-serve traditional OEM customers, which leaves room for a well-executed alternative.

The goal is not just to make things look polished. It is to help strong technical companies become easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to work with.

### Focused M&A scouting

I’m running an acquisition target search that I’ve deliberately narrowed into four core tracks:

- semiconductor precision heating and temperature sensing
- industrial electric heating
- temperature control instruments
- ceramic sealing and feedthroughs

The discipline now is the opposite of the earlier phase: not chasing every interesting category, but keeping a small set of tracks sharp enough that every target can be evaluated against clear criteria.

### Marketing, analytics, and the path from click to lead

I’m spending time on the connective tissue between marketing and actual business operations.

That includes Google Ads campaigns, conversion tags, lead form behavior, and the less glamorous details that decide whether a campaign can be measured honestly.

Recently this has included tightening the loop for industrial websites: making sure the right tags are installed, form submissions are trackable, and the marketing system reflects real buyer intent instead of vanity traffic.

### Website systems and international architecture

On the web side I’ve been working through how multilingual industrial sites should be structured — treating the URL as the single source of truth for language, so that search engines, visitors, and the content pipeline all agree on which page is which language.

The details are unglamorous (no auto-switching based on browser language, no redirect loops, no flicker), but they decide whether an international site is trustworthy or confusing.

### Business systems and internal tools

On the software side, I’m building and refining tools for project management, CRM, issue tracking, analytics, and internal operations.

I care about software that helps people see ownership, bottlenecks, follow-ups, and progress more clearly — not dashboards that only look good in screenshots.

### Infrastructure and self-hosted workflows

I also spend time on practical infrastructure: deployment, DNS, proxy routing, router configuration, load balancers, monitoring, and self-hosted systems.

This work is not glamorous, but I enjoy it. It is where software meets daily reliability: configuration, security, debugging, and making sure the systems I depend on behave predictably.

### AI-native workflows

I’m experimenting with AI agents, automation, activity recording, sandboxed execution, documentation workflows, and ways to turn daily work into reusable knowledge.

The question I keep coming back to is: how can AI become part of a real work system, instead of just being another chat box?

## What I’m thinking about

I’m thinking a lot about how traditional manufacturing companies can become more global, more digital, and more legible.

Many good companies already have real capability, but they do not always explain themselves well. That gap is interesting to me.

I’m also interested in the unglamorous middle layer of work: cleaning up data, improving workflows, debugging infrastructure, writing clearer explanations, and connecting one system to another.

That is where a lot of leverage hides.

Concretely right now, that means helping a company become a precision fluid control platform for extreme conditions — and making sure that story survives contact with real customers, real markets, and real operations.

Not theory for theory’s sake.

Useful things.
